Transparency

Resources & AI reflection

TSA allows generative AI in the Webmaster event if it is disclosed. Here is a summary of how our team used AI. The full reflection is in the Resources and AI Reflection PDF.

Tools we used

  • Claude (Anthropic): an AI assistant used for planning, organizing documentation, finding resources, and helping write and debug code.
  • Trello: our project timeline, set up like a Gantt chart, showing every task, who owned it, how to do it and when it was due. Trello itself is not a generative AI tool.
  • W3Schools: free tutorials we used, along with AI, to learn HTML, CSS and JavaScript.

How AI helped us document, track and research

  • Learning: We used AI alongside W3Schools to learn how to code and to understand what AI is and how it works, which is the topic of our site. We asked it to explain concepts and errors in plain language.
  • Organizing the project: We pasted the TSA design brief and rating form into Claude and asked it to turn them into a requirements checklist. We turned that list into tasks on our Trello timeline and assigned each one to a team member.
  • Keeping the work log: After meetings, we gave the AI our rough notes and asked it to sort them into clear work-log entries: task, owner, reviewer and status. We checked every entry against what actually happened before adding it to Trello and to our About page.
  • Tracking who did what: AI helped us plan how to lay out our Trello board like a Gantt chart, so every task had a clear owner, steps and a due date.
  • Finding resources: We asked AI to suggest credible kinds of sources for each topic, such as government frameworks, peer-reviewed studies and official style guides. We never cited a source the AI gave us without finding and reading the original ourselves. Several suggested links were outdated or broken, and we replaced them.
  • Building the website: AI helped generate and debug parts of the HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Team members reviewed, tested and edited the code, and we can explain how each part works.

What we did ourselves

  • Chose the concept, the three-module structure and the gamification system. TC and LK designed the look and outlined the content, and changed colors and styles in the code themselves.
  • Verified every fact, statistic and citation against original sources.
  • Tested the site across browsers and devices, and made the final decisions on all content.

Effectiveness

AI was most useful for organization and speed: turning messy notes into a clean work log, catching code bugs, and suggesting where to look for sources. It was least reliable for facts. It sometimes stated dates or details incorrectly, which is exactly why we built the “Spot the hallucination” lab. Using AI this way gave us more time for testing and polishing.

Ethical considerations

  • Honesty: We disclosed our AI use here and in the PDF, following TSA's rules on generative AI.
  • Accuracy: Every AI-suggested fact was checked by a team member against a credible source before publishing.
  • Privacy: We never entered personal information about students into AI tools. The site itself collects no personal data.
  • Originality: We did not use AI-generated images. All graphics are original.
